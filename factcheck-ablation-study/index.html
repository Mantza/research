Chatbots and fact checks: the answers

How Claude and ChatGPT answered 40 fact-checked claims, with fact-checking sites available and with them blocked.

How the experiment worked

Claims. 40 claims fact-checked between 19 and 22 October 2026 by members of the International Fact-Checking Network or the European Fact-Checking Standards Network. Each was asked in its original language: “Is this true? [claim]”, followed by “How do you know?”. The full study also covered Community Notes posts and control questions, not shown here.

Arms. Open web: the model's API with web search, nothing blocked. Fact-checkers blocked: the same, with up to 100 fact-checking domains blocked for that claim. Consumer app: the ChatGPT or Claude web app with default settings. Asked between 22 and 26 October 2026.

Wave 2. On 12 November 2026 the 12 image and video claims were asked again through the APIs with a frame from the original post attached: open web, fact-checkers blocked, and a third arm that also blocked sites that had republished the fact checks.

Coding. For fact-checked claims, two blind AI coders compared each answer with the full fact check: its verdict, whether its reasoning matched the fact check's key findings, whether it credited a fact-checker, and whether a fact check was its main source. The author reviewed the codes. Links are classed against the roster of fact-checking sites.

Reading the page. False the verdict matches the fact check; Mixed or True it does not; Can't tell no verdict. In the claim list, the four squares show open web then blocked, for Claude and ChatGPT.

Models: Claude (claude-opus-5-5) and ChatGPT (gpt-5.5). Answers are shown as recorded. Fact checks are linked, not reproduced.

    Part of “Demand for digital fact-checking in the age of AI”, a UNESCO fellowship report by Alexios Mantzarlis. Data: data.json.
